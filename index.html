    This is a teaching model, not measured data. It estimates the conditions SODIS relies on. It cannot show that any water has been disinfected or is safe to drink.

    How the model works
    • Sunlight rises and falls as a smooth curve between 6:00 and 18:00, peaking at about 1000 W/m² at noon on a sunny day. Clouds cut heat strongly but UV-A only partly (half cloudy keeps 85% of UV, overcast keeps 55%).
    • Heating uses an energy balance: sunlight absorbed by the bottle warms the water, and heat is lost to the surrounding air. Cloudier water absorbs a little more sunlight, so it can warm slightly faster.
    • Light inside the water follows the Beer–Lambert law. The more cloudiness and the wider the bottle, the less light reaches the far side.
    • Light dose is counted against the EAWAG guideline of about 555 Wh/m² of UV-A and violet light. Above 45 °C each unit counts 1.5 times, and above 50 °C it counts 3 times, to reflect the heat and light working together.
    • The shaded bottle gets 15% of the heat and 25% of the light that a sunlit bottle gets.
    • The numbers are tuned to be realistic in size, not precise. Our real experiment will test whether these patterns actually appear.
